TV

RJ Mitte, Walt Jr. on Breaking Bad, dies at 34

RJ Mitte, who played Walter White Jr. (“Flynn”) across all five seasons of Breaking Bad, died Monday in Brownsville, Texas, at 34. His representatives said he died by suicide. Mitte, who had cerebral palsy, drew on his own experience for the role and went on to become a prominent advocate for performers with disabilities, heading a philanthropic organization focused on the disabled community.

Bryan Cranston, his onscreen father, wrote that he was in “deep despair,” recalling that the two had been texting “just a few weeks ago — figuring out when we could meet for lunch,” and signed off, “I’ll miss you, son.” Aaron Paul said he was “heartbroken” and added, “to whoever happens to be reading this, please know you are loved, and you are not alone.” Dean Norris and Matt Jones also posted tributes. If you or someone you know is struggling, call or text 988 in the U.S.

First look: Austin Butler and Michael B. Jordan are Crockett and Tubbs in Miami Vice ’85

Universal released the first character images from Joseph Kosinski’s Miami Vice ’85 as cameras started rolling: Butler as Sonny Crockett in a gray suit, gun drawn, and Jordan as Ricardo Tubbs in a black leather trench. The reboot is billed as exploring “the glamour and corruption of mid-80’s Miami,” inspired by the pilot and first season of the Don Johnson–Philip Michael Thomas series.

The Top Gun: Maverick and F1 director has stacked the bench: Alden Ehrenreich, Whitney Peak, Camila Morrone, Danny Ramirez, John Leguizamo, Andrew Schulz, Edi Gathegi, Walton Goggins, André Holland and Michael Irby. It opens May 19, 2028. There’s no trailer yet, so the first-look stills are the best look for now.

Alex Gibney’s Musk goes nationwide Friday, and Elon is not taking it quietly

Gibney’s nearly four-hour documentary on the world’s richest man is in select New York and L.A. theaters now and expands nationwide Oct. 16 via Bleecker Street. It traces Musk from early Silicon Valley through SpaceX, Tesla and DOGE, with on-camera interviews including his father Errol Musk and his first wife. Musk declined to participate.

Musk has spent weeks calling Gibney an “idiot” and a “tinfoil hat crackpot” on X, and his lawyers have floated legal threats. The film has struggled to buy ads: X, TikTok, Meta and YouTube had all refused paid promotion by Sept. 25 (Meta and YouTube later reversed). There are also open questions about whether it will still air on HBO after the Paramount–Warner Bros. Discovery merger under David Ellison, son of Musk’s friend Larry Ellison.

Britney Spears drops “She’ll Never Be Me,” a 2001 vault song co-written with Justin Timberlake

Recorded for her 2001 album Britney, back when she and Timberlake were dating, the track is a kiss-off to an ex who moved on with a lookalike. It’s her first new release since 2023’s “Mind Your Business” with will.i.am, and it arrived with almost no fanfare: just a reel on her Facebook page, no label press release, and no word from Spears herself.

It’s the first of five vault tracks on Britney (25th Anniversary Edition), out Nov. 6, alongside “Am I a Sinner,” “When I Say So,” “My Big Secret” and a full version of the Pepsi jingle “Right Now (Taste the Victory).” Her team promises “more surprises every week” until then.
