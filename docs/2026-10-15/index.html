Streaming

Netflix cuts about 1,000 jobs, its biggest layoff ever

Co-CEOs Ted Sarandos and Greg Peters told staff on Wednesday that Netflix is letting go of roughly 6% of its workforce, with product, tech, legal and HR teams hit hardest. It’s the company’s deepest cut by far: its last major round, in 2022, eliminated about 450 jobs after it lost subscribers for the first time in a decade.

The memo insists “our business is strong and growing” and that Netflix is on track for its full-year forecast. But engagement growth has slowed, competition from YouTube and the legacy studios keeps rising, and the stock is down more than 30% over the past six months.

Celebrity

Russell Brand’s rape trial opens with prosecutors calling him a “ruthless” predator

Brand, 51, went on trial Wednesday at Southwark Crown Court in London, facing three counts of rape, three of sexual assault and one of indecent assault involving six women between 1999 and 2009. He has pleaded not guilty to all of them. The prosecution opened by describing him as a “ruthless and calculated” sexual predator.

On Thursday the jury of eight men and four women watched the first complainant’s police interview, in which she alleges Brand raped her in a hotel room at the 1999 Labour Party conference in Bournemouth. Brand told reporters on arrival that he felt “most blessed.” The allegations first surfaced in a 2023 Sunday Times and Channel 4 Dispatches investigation, and the trial is expected to run six to eight weeks.

TV

The Victorious spinoff Hollywood Arts is out on Netflix, and critics aren’t kind

Daniella Monet is back as Trina Vega, Tori’s perpetually struggling older sister, who returns to her old performing arts school as a wildly unqualified substitute teacher for a new class of students. Yvette Nicole Brown and Eric Lange return as Principal Helen and Sikowitz, and it’s the first trip back to the school since Victorious wrapped 13 years ago, this time without disgraced original creator Dan Schneider.

Variety calls it “a bland copy of shows from a past era” and a “near-carbon copy” of the original, while What’s on Netflix gave it one star as a “lifeless nostalgia grab.” Even Variety credits the young cast’s singing and comedy chops, though, and some reviewers like Monet’s warmer, more grown-up Trina.

Zach Bryan announces a 2027 stadium tour, with Soldier Field on May 8

The “Young Manhood and Other Donatable Organs” tour covers 23 shows in 19 stadiums across North America and Australia, opening April 28 in Minneapolis. The Chicago date is Saturday, May 8, 2027, at Soldier Field, with John Fogerty and Fey Fili opening. The artist presale starts Wednesday, Oct. 21, at 10 a.m. local time, and tickets go on sale to everyone Friday, Oct. 23.

It’s timed to his eight-song EP of the same name, out Friday, Oct. 16, with Lana Del Rey on “Lies” and Turnpike Troubadours on “Plainsman.” The EP isn’t streaming yet, so the Spotify link below goes to his artist page.
