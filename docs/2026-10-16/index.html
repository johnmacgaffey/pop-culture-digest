What’s actually dominating today
Friday’s heat: Zach Bryan’s new EP with Lana Del Rey and Turnpike Troubadours is out everywhere, Olivia Rodrigo and Chappell Roan dropped their campy “expectations” remix overnight, Street Fighter punches into theaters with $4 million in Thursday previews, and Al Pacino lands SAG-AFTRA’s Life Achievement Award.
Music

Zach Bryan’s Young Manhood and Other Donatable Organs is out now

The eight-song EP arrived Friday via Warner Records, led by the Lana Del Rey duet “Lies” and capped with fellow Oklahomans Turnpike Troubadours on “Plainsman.” It also folds in live-show favorites like “Robot Killed the Working Man” and “Backwards,” and it’s Bryan’s first new music since January’s Billboard 200–topping With Heaven On Top (and its acoustic companion three days later).

The drop lands a day after he unveiled the 2027 “Young Manhood and Other Donatable Organs” stadium tour — 23 shows across North America and Australia, with Chicago’s Soldier Field on May 8. Artist presale starts Oct. 21; general onsale is Oct. 23.

Music

Olivia Rodrigo and Chappell Roan drop the “expectations” remix — with David Byrne as game-show host

Thursday at 5 p.m. PT, Rodrigo released her first-ever remix: “expectations” featuring Chappell Roan, produced by Dan Nigro. It’s Roan’s first feature on another artist’s track. The original sits on Rodrigo’s Billboard 200–topping third album, you seem pretty sad for a girl so in love.

The Bardia Zeinali video casts them as rivals on “The Big Expectations Show,” a stack of ’80s/’90s game-show spoofs (including a Family Feud riff called Sister Squabble) hosted by Talking Heads’ David Byrne as fictional emcee Johnny Johnson, with a cameo from Nigro. Roan contributes a new verse aimed at an underwhelming female suitor.

Movies

Street Fighter opens with $4M Thursday previews; eyes a $30M+ weekend

Paramount/Legendary’s PG-13 Capcom adaptation, starring Noah Centineo and Andrew Koji, took in about $4 million from Thursday previews that started at 2 p.m. (plus a 7 p.m. fan showtime). Deadline says the $82 million co-production is tracking to open north of $30 million; critics have it at 47% on Rotten Tomatoes.

It’s a crowded mid-October frame: A24’s Elizabeth Holmes doc You Can See Everything (Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim) pulled an impressive ~$2M in previews and is climbing toward a possible $10M+ open; Focus’s Sense and Sensibility and 20th’s Whalefall each did about $1M Thursday.

Celebrity

Al Pacino will receive SAG-AFTRA’s Life Achievement Award

SAG-AFTRA named Pacino the 62nd recipient of its Life Achievement Award, citing his career and humanitarian work. He’ll accept the union’s highest honor at the 33rd Actor Awards on Feb. 28, 2027, at the Shrine Auditorium in Los Angeles; the ceremony streams live on Netflix.

President Sean Astin called him “an artist of extraordinary courage, intensity and heart.” Pacino’s résumé includes the Oscar for Scent of a Woman, nominations spanning The Godfather films through The Irishman, plus Tonys, Emmys, and prior Actor Awards.
