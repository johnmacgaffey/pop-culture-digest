Movies

Godzilla Minus Zero stomps toward a record $30M–$33M U.S. opening

After a $13.5 million Friday (including $6.7 million in previews) at 3,254 theaters, Takashi Yamazaki’s R-rated Toho sequel — distributed by GKids — is tracking a $30 million–$33 million domestic weekend. That would be the biggest opening ever for a live-action foreign-language film in North America, topping 2004’s Hero ($17.8 million), and by far GKids’ largest debut.

Audience love is deafening (96% on Rotten Tomatoes’ Popcornmeter; critics are at 91% Certified Fresh). It’s the first Japanese production filmed for Imax, and it builds on 2023’s Oscar-winning Godzilla Minus One, which opened to $11.4 million and finished as the top-grossing live-action Japanese movie here.

TV

Druski hosts SNL tonight — the first digital content creator ever to do it

Drew Desbordes — better known as Druski — takes Studio 8H at 11:30 p.m. ET for Season 52’s fifth episode, the first time an online creator has hosted Saturday Night Live. His viral characters and long-form YouTube sketches (including the 24-million-view dating-show parody Coulda Been Love) made him the obvious pick for the milestone.

Musical guest is The Strokes, back for their fifth SNL appearance while touring behind July’s Reality Awaits. The episode streams on Peacock tomorrow.

Music

My Chemical Romance’s 20-year-old The Black Parade finally hits No. 1

The Oct. 23 20th Anniversary Deluxe Edition shot 139–1 on the Billboard 200 chart dated Nov. 7 — the band’s first No. 1 album after nearly 25 years. The reissue moved about 78,000 equivalent album units across more than 20 physical variants, finally clearing the No. 2 peak the original 2006 LP hit behind the Hannah Montana soundtrack.

Olivia Rodrigo, whose new album it beat, posted pink hearts and a clip from their Citi Field show. The Long Live the Black Parade tour wraps later this month.

Streaming

Zoey Deutch’s Gail Daughtry and the Celebrity Sex Pass hits Netflix today

David Wain’s R-rated Sundance comedy — Zoey Deutch as a Kansas hairdresser who flies to L.A. to cash in her celebrity hall-pass with Jon Hamm (playing himself), with John Slattery along for the ride — arrives on Netflix U.S. today, exactly 120 days after its modest theatrical run.

Critics liked it more than audiences did in theaters (78% on Rotten Tomatoes; ~$1.4 million domestic). Keep an eye out for cameos from Jennifer Aniston, Paul Rudd, Elizabeth Banks, Henry Winkler and “Weird Al” Yankovic.
