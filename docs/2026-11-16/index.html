Music

Raye leads the Grammy nominations with 11, and women take seven of the eight Album of the Year slots

The Recording Academy read out nominees in all 100 categories on a livestream this morning, the first time it has announced every category live. Variety’s Chris Willman says the big surprise was Raye coming out on top with 11 nominations, including Album of the Year for This Music May Contain Hope. and Record and Song of the Year for “Where Is My Husband!” Rosalía is next with nine. Ella Langley, Miranda Lambert, Olivia Rodrigo and Phoebe Bridgers have seven each, and Bruno Mars, Leon Thomas and Olivia Dean have six.

The Album of the Year nominees are Raye, Rosalía’s Lux (Complete Works), Bridgers’ Lost Weekend, Dean’s The Art of Loving, Langley’s Dandelion, Mars’ The Romantic, Rodrigo’s You Seem Pretty Sad for a Girl So in Love and Taylor Swift’s The Life of a Showgirl. That’s seven women and one man, and Song of the Year splits the same way. Langley is also up for Best New Artist, so she could win all four top categories. Swift got five nominations, with “I Knew It, I Knew You” in Song of the Year. Beyoncé became the first artist ever to reach 100 Grammy nominations, with “Morning Dew (Donk)” in Best R&B Solo Performance. Harry Styles got no nominations at all, and Noah Kahan got just one. The Grammys air Feb. 7 on ABC and stream on Disney+ and Hulu.

Glenn Close finally gets her Oscar at the Governors Awards

Close has eight Oscar nominations and no wins, which ties Peter O’Toole for the most acting nominations without a win. On Sunday night in Hollywood, the Academy gave her an honorary Oscar. Her Dangerous Liaisons co-star John Malkovich presented it, opening with a list of her best on-screen deaths, from the chandelier in Mars Attacks! to the bathtub in Fatal Attraction. Close, 79, cried through a standing ovation. She talked about growing up “under the thumb of a restrictive cult” from age 7 to 22. On the losses, she said, “I have always felt that the great honor is to be in the room. Over the past 43 years, I have been in the room eight times.”

Ridley Scott, 88, also got an honorary Oscar. He accepted by video after what his reps called a minor medical procedure, and dedicated the award to his late brother, Tony. Presenting it, Matt Damon told the story of Scott talking him into The Martian with five words: “No one gives a shit.” Floyd Norman, 91, widely recognized as Disney’s first Black animator, got one too: “On occasion, even movies have happy endings. And this special evening, here’s my happy ending.” Killer Films’ Christine Vachon and Pamela Koffler became the first all-female producing team to win the Thalberg Award. Neil Patrick Harris sang from Hedwig and the Angry Inch in their honor. This isn’t a movie, so there’s no trailer. The links below are the Academy’s official videos.
