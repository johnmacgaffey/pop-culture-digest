Robert Pattinson is the new James Bond, and the internet has thoughts

Amazon MGM Studios named Pattinson the seventh 007 on Monday and posted a photo of him in a tux holding a pistol. He replaces Daniel Craig. Denis Villeneuve, who just directed him in Dune: Part Three, will direct, Peaky Blinders creator Steven Knight is writing the script, and Amy Pascal and David Heyman are producing. The BBC says the film is due in 2028, but there’s no title or release date yet. “To be entrusted with 007 is the honour of a lifetime,” said Pattinson, 40. Villeneuve said he has “the timeless soul of 007 and brings a spirit that is completely new.”

The reaction has been loud. Brad Pitt called it “a great choice” and pitched himself as the villain: “Get Denis Villeneuve on the flip phone.” Ralph Fiennes, who plays M, was live on SiriusXM when the news broke: “I’m sorry, I’m in love with Daniel Craig still.” Piers Morgan called it “a bad call” because “Batman can’t be Bond too.” Uri Geller asked on X whether the photo was AI because of the thumb on the gun. A Community Note explained it’s a standard two-handed thumbs-forward grip. There’s no trailer yet, so the first link below is the official announcement video.

First reactions call Fincher’s Cliff Booth sequel “wildly entertaining”

The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth premiered Monday night at the TCL Chinese Theatre. It’s David Fincher’s film of a Quentin Tarantino script, with Brad Pitt back in his Oscar-winning role. Ted Sarandos introduced it and joked that he greenlit it from Fincher’s text: “Written by Quentin Tarantino. Directed by David Fincher. Starring Brad Pitt.” His reply was “Yes.” The story is set in 1977, eight years after the Manson night, and Cliff now works as a Hollywood fixer. Elizabeth Debicki, Scott Caan, Carla Gugino, Yahya Abdul-Mateen II and Peter Weller co-star.

Reactions on X were almost all raves. Variety’s Clayton Davis called it “a wild, hilarious hangout movie” and said it’s Pitt’s best work since the original: “If the 2019 film was a love letter to L.A., this one is a full-on sonnet.” TheWrap’s Drew Taylor said Tarantino and Fincher “are like peanut butter and chocolate.” Germain Lussier called it his “favorite movie of the year and it’s not even close.” IndieWire says a few scenes look “destined to go very viral.” It has a two-week IMAX-only run starting Nov. 25 and comes to Netflix on Dec. 23.
