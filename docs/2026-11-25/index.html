Movies

The final Avengers: Doomsday trailer hits 563 million views in 24 hours, Disney’s best ever

Marvel released the final Avengers: Doomsday trailer on Monday, Nov. 23, and Deadline reports it drew 563 million views in its first 24 hours. That makes it the most-viewed Disney trailer ever, beating the Doomsday teaser’s 503 million, and the second-most-viewed movie trailer of all time behind Spider-Man: Brand New Day’s 718.6 million. It also took the top three spots on YouTube’s movie trending chart and logged close to a million social mentions on day one.

The three-minute trailer opens with Chris Evans’ Steve Rogers and Hayley Atwell’s Peggy Carter at home with their son, who’s now a teenager, until Pedro Pascal’s Reed Richards and the Fantastic Four show up asking, “Are you Steve Rogers?” Robert Downey Jr.’s Doctor Doom closes it out with “All of you have lived stolen lives. But now you must give them back.” Hailee Steinfeld, whose casting Marvel announced Sunday, also appears. The Russo brothers’ film opens Dec. 18.

Celebrity

Vanity Fair’s Taylor Swift exposé, “The Mastermind,” lands with a thud

On Monday, Vanity Fair published May Jeong’s “The Mastermind,” a long, critical profile that never names Taylor Swift. It calls her “The Star,” Charli xcx “The Brat” and Scooter Braun “The Manager.” Jeong says she interviewed more than 100 people, but much of the piece rests on anonymous sources. One, “The Exec,” compares Swift to Mean Girls’ Regina George. Former classmates also dispute her stories of being bullied, and one remembers that she owned Ugg boots in three colors.

Most of the reaction was mockery. The Uggs line became the internet’s favorite joke, and Variety’s chief music critic, Chris Willman, wrote that Monday wasn’t a bad day for Swift but “a bad day for journalism.” Some readers said it made them like her more. Page Six reports that Jeong limited comments on her Instagram, and a source told the outlet Swift isn’t “losing sleep” over what it called a “collection of old grievances and unflattering anecdotes.” Swift’s reps haven’t commented publicly.

Music

Don Toliver’s Manchester show is evacuated after a substance is released in the crowd

More than 12,000 people were evacuated from Manchester’s Co-op Live arena on Sunday night, Nov. 22, after an “unknown substance” was released near the end of Don Toliver’s set. Police declared a major incident. One person went to the hospital and 88 others went through decontamination at the scene. Fans told the BBC about a burning feeling in their throats, people “choking” and some “coughing up blood.” Police first thought it was tear gas, and the substance still hasn’t been identified.

Greater Manchester Police now say the release may be linked to robberies. Officers got nine reports of stolen jewelry during the show and say similar robberies were reported at two of Toliver’s European concerts. No arrests have been made. Toliver moved his London dates to Dec. 14 (Wembley Arena) and Dec. 15 (The O2), and Live Nation is refunding Manchester ticket holders. On Instagram, Toliver asked fans to stop bringing substances to his shows: “It ain’t cool.”

Movies

Sunrise on the Reaping sets a franchise Tuesday record as Hexed and Focker-In-Law open for Thanksgiving

The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping made an estimated $16 million on Tuesday, according to Deadline. That’s a franchise record for the day, beating Catching Fire, and Lionsgate’s best Tuesday ever. It brings the five-day total to $148.2 million, after Sunday estimates were adjusted to a $119.3 million opening. Variety projects $80 million to $90 million from Wednesday through Sunday, enough to win Thanksgiving easily. Lionsgate CEO Adam Fogelson says the studio is talking with Suzanne Collins about another film.

Two wide releases open today. Disney Animation’s original Hexed, with Hailee Steinfeld, Rashida Jones and Jodie Foster, cost more than $200 million and has mixed reviews. Universal’s Focker-In-Law brings back Ben Stiller and Robert De Niro and adds Ariana Grande, but sits at 28% on Rotten Tomatoes. Variety has each tracking for $40 million to $45 million over the five days. Netflix’s Brad Pitt sequel The Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth also plays on about 250 IMAX screens. Domestic ticket sales are running 20% ahead of 2025, and the year is on track to be the first since Covid to pass $10 billion.
